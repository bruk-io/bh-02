## Description

<!-- SECTION:DESCRIPTION:BEGIN -->

Spec first: decision-185 in SPEC.md section 10 (after decision-184, whose residual bullet now says it is amended): one verified dial, Runtime.connect / Process.connect (the only documented way to dial a reserved channel; endpoint raw skips the check), Runtime.ready is that dial closed at once (False while nothing listens, EndpointRefused raised, never waited out); lstat before connecting (a socket, not a link); after connecting the peer (Linux SO_PEERCRED, darwin LOCAL_PEERPID) must be in the started process's own process group, its leader holding its recorded identity read after the peer's group (UNKNOWN stamp refuses). Why the group and not descent or the jail's processes: brig's control is group-shaped, every host start leads its own group and bwrap emits no --new-session, so the workload and its forks stay in it; a co-tenant is another group and is refused; descent breaks for orphans without a pid namespace and needs the process table walked; a listener that setsid()s is refused (safe direction). Nested pid namespace: the kernel numbers the peer in the reader's namespace; a peer it can't number is pid 0, refused. Where Linux has SO_PEERPIDFD (6.5+, used here: kernel 6.18) the listener's pidfd must be alive before and after the group read, so a pid reused after its listener left can't vouch; without it (older kernels) and on darwin (no pidfd) that reuse is a named residual; darwin's path is written from <sys/un.h> and unverified (no darwin host). A platform with no peer reading refuses every dial. Grades unchanged; Jail.wait_ready(channel) for a declared endpoint still connects as found until TASK-0079.32; bh-02's owners still dial raw until TASK-0079.31 moves them to Process.connect.
